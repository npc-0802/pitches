What a disciplined demand engine is worth in private-jet and custom-travel bookings

US visits to tcsworldtravel.com fell 68% between late June and mid-September as paid search ($74K in June, $3K in August) and display were wound down. 41% of the paid clicks had come from other travel brands' names, and most display visits were mobile and gone in under 30 seconds. So the plan restarts only the high-intent half of paid search, then builds the organic and AI layer that doesn't switch off with the budget. The unit of value is a booking, not a visit: one seat on the 28 Nov departure is $175K. Pick a tier, then replace our placeholder rates with TCS's own.

What doing nothing costs

Against June–July, about 11,600 high-intent visits a month are gone: 7,875 on-intent paid visits, 1,536 commercial organic clicks and about 2,166 AI-assistant referrals. At the rates above that is $608K of trip revenue a month, or one booking every 9 days. Buying those visits back in Google Ads at TCS's own $4.24 would cost about $49K a month. Organic traffic is down 53% since March (Ahrefs), about 12% a month, and AI Overviews now sit above 95 of the 101 tracked TCS rankings. Every month the plan waits pushes back $721K of trip revenue at run-rate. And seats on the 28 Nov departure can't be sold once the jet leaves.

Formula: high-intent visits added = (paid budget ÷ $4.24 − 1,502 on-intent paid visits today) + organic and AI visits added. Extra enquiries a month = (today + added) × enquiry rate × (1 + lift) − today × enquiry rate; × close rate = extra bookings; × average booking value × 12 = trip revenue a year. Gross profit after media = revenue × margin − paid budget × 12, before agency fees. The Ads cost of the organic and AI visits is what they would cost to buy, not what they earn, and is never added to revenue. Every revenue figure uses placeholder rates until TCS shares its own.
