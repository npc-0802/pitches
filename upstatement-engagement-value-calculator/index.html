What waiting costs

Upstatement's inbound is healthy and growing: form fills are up by 150–200 over the last few years. But that growth comes from people who already know the name. Its 10 service searches sent 6 clicks in a year from page two of Google, and it runs no paid media. Search ads can reach the rest within weeks of tracking going live, so every month without ads and the plan pushes back about $144K of new-engagement fees at run-rate, $69K of it from ads alone. Meanwhile 59,260 agency searches a month go to the studios and listicles on page one, AI engines name Upstatement in 4% of higher-ed answers against Carnegie's 36%, and public universities are scoping accessibility-driven redesigns ahead of the 26 April 2027 ADA Title II deadline. A flagship site that goes to a competitor isn't re-tendered for years.

Formula: ads: Google Ads budget ÷ cost per click × ad fill rate × 12 = form fills a year. Organic: (agency-seeking visits × agency fill rate + research visits × research fill rate + added AI visits × (1 + arrivals by name) × AI fill rate) × 12. Both: form fills × fill-to-qualified = qualified leads; × qualified-to-won = won engagements; × average fee × engagements per client = fees added a year. Added AI visits = 93 a month today × 50% from unbranded answers × (new share ÷ 9.7% − 1). Search visits are all incremental: Upstatement ranks for none of these terms today, and ads appear above page-two listings it barely gets clicks from. LinkedIn and Meta spend is counted in media but not in fees. The win rate and average fee are placeholders until Upstatement shares its own.
